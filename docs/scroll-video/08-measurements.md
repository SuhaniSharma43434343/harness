# 8. Measurements

Measured on 2026-11-13 by `tests/build/scroll-video.test.mjs` on the development PC (Windows 11, 12 logical cores, Intel Iris Xe), in **headless Playwright Chromium**. These numbers describe that browser on that machine. They are not claims about phones, Safari, or a real Chrome window: those rows belong in `docs/device-log.md` and are **not run**.

| What | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Frame accuracy, forward and reverse (pizza, car, particles: 21 + 20 steps each; pizza-alt, outfit, building, product, journey, heavy) | Every settled step showed the frame for the scroll position (decoded time code within one frame), and the scene text matched that frame. 0 misses. |
| Pinning | Stage top within 1 px of the viewport top at every sampled step. |
| Seek time (the engine's own `seeked` timing, last 64 seeks) | pizza, 0.167 s keyframes: median **12 ms**, max 26 ms. particles, 0.2 s: median **20 ms**, max 46 ms. car, one keyframe: median **83 ms**, max **370 ms**. |
| Time to settle after a scroll step (includes the deliberate 120 ms smoothing) | pizza p50/p95 forward 323/474 ms, reverse 329/728 ms. car forward 469/663 ms, reverse 433/1043 ms. particles forward 423/628 ms, reverse 415/543 ms. |
| Slow seeking switches to frames | `car-auto` (one keyframe, frames at 15 fps, a 1 ms test budget so the switch is certain): switches after 8 or more seeks, then frames are exact both ways. | Resize | Desktop to phone at mid-track: same progress (within 0.02) and scene; source swapped to the phone cut and back. |
| Layout shift | CLS 0.000 on load and while scrolling. |

## Under load

`npm test` runs every test file in parallel, so the browser test shares the CPU with seven other Astro builds and browsers. In one such run the pizza seek median rose from 12 ms to 111 ms (max 334 ms) and three forward steps took over 4 s to settle; frame accuracy still held. On another run, with other applications holding the CPU at 50 to 60%, single seeks took up to 5.8 s. The tests therefore assert correctness under a 20 s settle cap and report timings as diagnostics. A busy device scrubs later, not wrongly.

## What this says

- Keyframe spacing decides scrubbing cost: a clip with one keyframe seeks about 7 times slower (median) than one with a keyframe every 4 frames, with spikes over 300 ms in reverse. That is why `add-video` warns above 0.5 s and prints a `-g` re-encode, and why the frames renderer exists.
- Smoothing, not decoding, dominates settle time on a well-encoded clip: seeks are 12 to 20 ms; the remaining time is the 120 ms exponential follow, which is the intended feel (`smoothingMs: 0` gives a hard scrub).

## Not measured

- H.264 MP4 playback (Playwright's Chromium has no H.264 decoder); Safari, Firefox, real Chrome and Edge windows; real phones and their GPUs; frame times on a mid-range phone; battery. Fill `docs/device-log.md` by hand before a client release.
- Real AI-generated footage. The fixtures are drawn test clips with the same container and codec properties; the first client clip should be run through `forge scroll-video probe` and the stills check.
