# Saltline Chocolate: client notes (sample client for testing)

This is an invented test client. Everything below counts as the client's own statements.

- Two people: Ines Duarte (chocolate maker, formerly a marine biologist) and Sam Okafor (runs everything else). Workshop in Astoria, Oregon, on the Columbia River estuary.
- The product: the Tideline bar. 72% dark chocolate from a single farm, with flakes of sea salt the two of them harvest by hand from Oregon coast seawater.
- The cacao comes from Finca Las Mareas in Ecuador, a 12-hectare family farm in Los Rios run by the Cedeno family. Direct purchase at $6.40 per kilo.
- The process: beans fermented 6 days and dried at the farm, roasted in Astoria in a converted coffee roaster, stone-ground for 72 hours, aged 45 days.
- The salt: they collect seawater at Cannon Beach on the spring tides, evaporate it slowly in shallow pans in a greenhouse over about 3 weeks, and rake the flakes by hand.
- The idea behind it: the bar is about two tides. The Guayas river tide that reaches the farm in Ecuador, and the Pacific tide at Cannon Beach. Ines noticed the chocolate tastes of the place where the fresh water meets the salt.
- Taste: dark red fruit first, then roasted nut, then the salt arrives late. Ines's phrase: "The salt comes in last, like the tide."
- Batch 001: 1,200 bars. 70 g each. $14 a bar. Ships from 15 February 2027, in the US only. Each bar has a handwritten batch number.
- Reserve by email: reserve@saltline.example. No payment until it ships.
- Packaging: uncoated paper, printed with a tide table for the day the bar was moulded.
- No photography exists yet. The look they imagine: tide charts, contour lines, salt crystals, the colour of wet slate and dark cacao, with one line of copper. They have asked for illustration rather than stock photos.
- They dislike: chocolate-porn close-ups, gold foil, the word "artisanal", and anything that looks like a luxury hotel.
